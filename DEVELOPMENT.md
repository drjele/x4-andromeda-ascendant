## Installation and publishing helpers

`install.sh` and `publish.sh` both source `lib/find_x4.sh`. The library searches usual Steam roots and additional library folders. `X4_PATH`, `X_TOOLS_PATH`
and `PROTON_PATH` override discovery. Proton Experimental is preferred when found; otherwise the helper uses the last matching Proton directory it encounters.

Installation prepares a complete copy before replacing the extension directory. Both helpers require Python 3.10+ to parse the manifest safely. Unknown install arguments and unsafe target paths are rejected. A per-extension directory lock prevents simultaneous helper operations; temporary copies live outside `extensions/`. A failed or interrupted operation restores the previous installation. SIGKILL or power loss cannot run cleanup: inspect the printed transaction paths or hidden `.<extension-id>.*` directories in the game root before removing a stale lock. Refresh it after edits; X4 enumerates real extension directories, so a symlink does not substitute for installation. Restart the game after installing or removing.

Publishing stages a separate copy inside the game's extensions directory. The repository keeps its readable extension id; `steam/workshop-id` holds the numeric Workshop id. The helper changes only the staged manifest and runs the interactive WorkshopTool. An exit trap restores the exact previous installation on success, error or a handled interrupt; if none existed, it removes the staged copy. On Linux it runs WorkshopTool through Proton and maps paths through drive Z. Workshop ids must be complete numeric ids, not strings from which digits can be extracted. Tests use a fake WorkshopTool and never upload anything.

## Mesh conversion

`soase_import.py` reads Ironclad's text mesh format, an indentation-nested key-value tree whose blocks repeat by name. It refuses binary meshes; convert those with ConvertX first. Sins uses X right, Y up and Z forward, so the converter maps `(x, y, z)` to `(-x, z, y)`: swapping the two axes alone would mirror the model, and negating X restores the handedness. This transform has determinant +1, so triangle winding is preserved, and the V coordinate is flipped from the DirectX convention OBJ does not use. Hardpoint orientations use a basis change (`T R T^-1`), so identity remains identity. Positions and normals use `T`.

`andromeda_import.py` imports the OBJ with `forward_axis="Y"` and `up_axis="Z"` so Blender's own axis conversion stays out of the way, then scales, centres, decimates and places connections. Keep `andromeda_import.py` and `andromeda_turrets.py` together; run their files through Blender so the shared extraction module can be resolved.

## Reproducing the pipeline

README.md documents the four stages end to end. What belongs here are the traps, which cost the most time to find:

- The exporter refuses any `.blend` whose path lacks the literal string `[assets]`, and reports the refusal only as an info message alongside a success message.
- `XUConverter.exe` is the tool the community guide calls "P1 Converter Local". It processes the initial source files and then watches the folder; use a fresh process and fresh input/output directories to avoid stale conversions.
- Under the tested Proton setup it exited silently with no output. Two DLLs are the cause: Proton's builtin `CONCRT140`
  lacks `?PPLParallelForEventGuid@Concurrency` and `mfc140` is missing. Plain Wine with
  `winetricks -q vcrun2022` runs it, and it then prints a usage line proving it takes arguments.
- Extension files are read off a case-sensitive filesystem. The localisation file must be
  `t/0001-l044.xml`, lowercase, as it is inside the game's own catalogues.
- This pipeline uses gzipped DXT5 textures with mipmaps. Earlier uncompressed DDS experiments did not render correctly; other supported X4 texture formats were not exhaustively tested.

## Selectable exclusive equipment and hull appearance

`libraries/equipmentcompatibilities.xml` registers `andromeda`. Ship engine/weapon mounts and their component bindings use that tag without `standard` or `mandatory`. The custom lance component is a separate copy of the Ravager's geometry-free laser emitter, with the original Kha'ak beam macro and effects. The original vanilla weapon component is not patched. Emitter orientation is identity, as on the Ravager; its firing axis differs from a turret mounting normal.

The vanilla ship configuration menu treats weapons as individual slots, not turret/engine groups. Keep both weapon connections ungrouped. Compatibility and macro identities are tested locally; previously fitted equipment is not replaced in saves.

Each of the 54 standard turret and shield mounts gets a generated pad in the hull mesh. `add_pad` casts rays along the mount normal across a rounded-square footprint (a superellipse aligned with ship-forward) sized from the vanilla Argon socket plus a rim. The flat top sits at the 80th percentile of the hull heights under it, so it stays low on curved surfaces. A fillet of five rings then flares out to 1.5 times the footprint along an S-curve, blending each ring towards the hull height beneath it, so the shoulder is rounded and the foot follows the original surface into the hull. Each ring's hull heights are smoothed around the ring so the fillet does not copy every crease, and the last ring keeps the lower of the smoothed and raw heights so no gap opens. Where a foot would run past a hull edge, the flank is shortened in that direction until it lands on hull. Turret pads are also turned about their normal, in 7.5° steps, to the angle whose footprint and fillet cover the evenest hull, which keeps them off ribs and sharp ridges; a mirrored pad takes its partner's angle reversed, and a centreline pad may only be straight or diagonal. Shield pads stay aligned with the ship, matching their shields. No pad is built where the hull under the socket is already level: if a circle of 80% of the footprint radius varies by less than 10% of that radius (at least 0.3 m), the mount sits directly on the original surface, which keeps existing details such as the octagonal plate on the spine and the teardrops on the deck intact. Fillets are at most 18 m wide, so XL shield pads stop at their own footprint instead of spreading 50 m past it. A comparison of the original hull with the padded hull found pads through thin fins and over details; sliding pads sideways was tried and dropped because it moved them onto other features. `con_turret_006` and the `_103`/`_104` pair were later moved to the nearest level, clear site in their zone, with their shields, so they need no pad. No level on-axis site for a large turret exists under the bow, where the keel forms a V, so `con_turret_002` moved about 820 m aft to the level bottom plate at midships, ahead of the spine-bottom large turret; it keeps its `group_front_down_mid` identity. Zone mounts must also sit on the surface seen from their side: a top mount on the first surface hit from above, a bottom mount on the first hit from below, except the spine-top turret under the deck and the deck-underside mounts, which were requested there. Sixteen turrets and their shields that the first zone fit had put on arms passing under the wing panels, under the aft body or under the deck branches were moved to visible level sites in the same zone. The connection sits on the pad top. Pads are built from the hull surface before they are added, so ray casts and fixed-mount placement are not affected by other pads.

`ZONE_MOUNTS` adds 118 turrets (15 large, 103 medium) and 133 medium shields chosen zone by zone with the user: nose, deck (the dorsal bridge above the hull), spine, aft body, arms, flanks and rear loops, each top and bottom. Each zone and side is its own group, so it can be equipped separately. Every medium turret has one medium shield beside it and every large turret two, in the same group, which also satisfies the rule that every turret group carries a shield. Sites were picked offline from flat, clear, outward-facing hull with room above, away from existing mounts, and mirrored. Under the bow the five medium turrets form a V whose apex sits on the keel line. The spine-top large turret sits under the deck and is screened above by it. Zone mounts are listed in `LAYERED`: `HullSurface.at` walks every surface on the vertical line and takes the outward one nearest the seed height, because decks and arms stack several layers. Existing mounts keep first-hit placement, so their names, tags, groups and positions are unchanged; the lighter small-pad geometry moved them at most 0.15 m. Pads with half-extents under 20 m use 16 segments and three fillet rings to keep LOD0 near 53,000 triangles. `package_assets.py` accepts standard turret additions `con_turret_037` to `_154` and medium shields up to `con_shieldgen_m_147`.

The hull is made exactly mirror-symmetric before anything is placed on it. The source mesh is symmetric to a centimetre almost everywhere (one small detail under the hull deviated by up to 15.6 m), so `symmetrize` cuts it on the centre plane, keeps the right half, mirrors it, reverses the mirrored faces (mirroring keeps vertex order, which would point them inward), merges the centre seam, drops duplicate centre faces and loose vertices. Each decimated LOD is symmetrized the same way, since Blender's symmetric decimation was not exact. A ray that runs exactly along the mirrored seam can slip between the halves, so `HullSurface.cast` retries 1 cm to either side. The four integrated turret stations, taken from the source before symmetrizing, are averaged with their mirrored partners, moving them by under a centimetre. `check_blender.py` asserts that every `part_main` LOD mirrors onto itself within 5 cm; the measured deviation is zero.

Mounts are mirror-symmetric about the ship's centre plane. Left-hand mounts are reflections of their right-hand partners, including pad height, and centreline mounts have their position and normal forced onto the plane, because the source mesh is not exactly symmetric. `check_blender.py` asserts the symmetry on the saved scene. Connection names, tags and groups are unchanged; only their positions move.

Shields use an explicit orthonormal frame: local Blender Z is the surface normal and local Y is ship-forward projected onto the mounting plane. This corresponds to the native shield mesh's longitudinal Z axis after X4 export and avoids the arbitrary roll of `to_track_quat` on sloped hull faces.

The hull uses the mod's own tileable panel maps through the p1 shader, with a 120 m box UV projection. The base-game `gen_p2_hulltexture_02` used before is a trim sheet of technical strips, not hull plating, so box-projecting it gave a random patchwork. No Egosoft textures are redistributed; the base-game `gen_p2_detail_01_normal` is only referenced as a detail normal.

`scripts/make_hull_textures.py` (run with Blender for numpy) splits a 2048 px torus into grid-aligned plates of 4 to 20 m, adds thin seams, a few inset hatches with bolts and very small tone and smoothness variation, and writes diffuse, normal, smoothness and metal maps as PNG previews and gzipped DXT5 DDS with full mip chains through ImageMagick. Larger plates and low variation matter: per-plate smoothness differences read as a checkerboard on polished metal. The base-game normal maps store X and Y in red and green with blue zero, so the shader rebuilds Z; the generated normal follows that, with green in the DirectX convention. Whether X4's green axis matches has not been checked in-game.

The shipped maps come from `art/hull_panels_albedo.png` and `art/hull_panels_height.png`, generated by Codex (image generation, driven through Mode A of `drjele/claude/shared/collab/PROTOCOL.md`) in the style of the mod preview: angled plates, vents, hatches. Codex generated 1254 px and upscaled to 2048 px, corrected the tile edges so opposite edges match exactly, and derived the height from the albedo. Build them with `blender -b --python scripts/make_hull_textures.py -- <output dir> art`. That path softens the height by a 1.5 px tileable blur and uses a weaker normal (strength 2), because the one-pixel steps of a derived height glinted like scratches on polished metal; smoothness and metal fall in recessed seams. Without the `art` argument the script still produces the older procedural plates.

`hull_shades` bakes the vertex colour the panel albedo is multiplied by: a cool silver base, darkened by up to half from ambient occlusion (16 hemisphere rays to 150 m), up to 12% more on downward faces, and to 60% on mount pads, which `add_pad` marks with a temporary face attribute. The box projection uses |x|, so the panel pattern is mirrored across the centre plane like the hull.

The hull carries two finishes, as in the series stills: polished silver (`andromeda_hull`) and satin gunmetal with blue circuit tracery (`andromeda_hull_dark`, Smoothness 0.55, Metallness 0.5). `dark_faces` gives gunmetal to the side wings only: faces more than 130 m off the centre plane, more than 100 m from either arm path, outside the aft loops and whose vertex normals look up less than 0.35; the arms, the fused tips, the loops, the keel and the upper shells stay silver. Gunmetal faces take a darker vertex base, and their occlusion shade is raised to the power 1.8 so recesses fall toward black. `make_hull_textures.py` also writes `andromeda_dark_diff` (the panel albedo dimmed to 55% and crossed by a few wide traces on a 256 px grid that turn by 45 degrees and end in round pads, drawn with wrap-around so the tile stays seamless) and `andromeda_dark_glow`, the same traces as a faint glow (`color_glowStr` 0.25) that carries red too, since the engine's glow channel is still unconfirmed. The dark material takes the slot after the two light materials, so `without_strips` treats only the slots between as strips.

The livery follows the series (wiki stills and Codex's annotated crops, kept outside the repository): a ribbed red band on the inner top of each great arc, from where it leaves the dorsal structure to about half way to the bow tip; a cyan grille on each aft-loop bar and a cyan oval light on each loop's outer lower corner; and a cyan crescent grille under the central hull. Face selection could not draw thin continuous bands on the coarse, curved hull, so `add_strips` builds them as geometry. `crest_line` walks the ship in 10 m stations, looks down on the hull, starts on the widest outboard run and then takes the highest point within 40 m sideways of the previous crest, stopping where it jumps to another structure; strands of a loop merge into one run seen from above, which is why the search stays local. Those crests are only faired now (`smooth_crests`); the lights are placed from the matched reference cameras instead. Overlaying renders of this hull on the wiki stills, with cameras fitted to each still (silhouette overlap on LOD3 plus landmarks; the "orthographic" sheets turned out to be perspective renders from 2.6 to 6 km), and casting the stills' red and cyan pixels back onto the hull showed where the lights belong. The red band lies on the top of the arc, turned toward the inside (60 to 80 degrees from the inward horizontal), so it shows from above and from ahead; its run, from the root to about half the arc, was already right. `arc_top_stations` follows the rebuilt arc's own path from 7% to 52% of its length and drops onto the top a quarter of the local arc width inboard of the centreline, clear of the rib; the band narrows from 70 m to 2 m. The cyan lights are `CYAN_PATCHES` between two points of the right half, found the same way: a grille on the forward face of each aft-loop bar and an oval light on the outer lower corner of each loop, each with a width profile (an ellipse, or a box with rounded ends). `emit_strip` averages points and normals over ±80 m, resamples every 2 m, keeps the cross direction from flipping and lays rows of seven samples, each snapped to the nearest hull point and lifted along the hull normal there (middle 0.4 m, edges sunk 0.2 m), so the strip follows the contour rather than the smoothed chord; an earlier projection onto a strip plane left parts floating up to 19 m off curved arcs. Triangles under 0.01 m² are dropped, because neighbouring samples can snap onto the same point. Rows alternate lit, lit, lit, dark to read as transverse ribs, and every triangle is turned outward on its own, because bent quads split by the exporter could flip. The belly grille follows a crescent across the underside at y 220 m, 160 m across and 26 m wide, centred on the axis. Before anything is placed, `round_dorsal` splits the dorsal edges once (above 120 m, within 250 m of the axis, forward of y −400 m) and relaxes them with Taubin steps that keep volume, holding open source edges so they do not pull apart, and `smooth_crests` fairs the hull within 30 m of the crest lines the same way; the hull is symmetrized again afterwards. Mounts on the rounded deck moved by up to 9 m; names, tags and groups are unchanged. Strip faces are marked like pads and take the strip colour in the vertex colour. Glow test (untested in-game): lit ribs use `andromeda_lights_red` and `andromeda_lights_cyan`, copies of the hull material with `color_glow_map` and `color_glowStr` 4.0 (the property names come from the base-game material library, where a `glowtest` material uses `color_glow_map`); dark gaps stay on the hull material. The base-game glow maps carry intensity only in the red channel, so the two 64 px glow maps are a diagnostic: the red one is pure red, the cyan one has red at zero. If only the red ribs glow in game, X4 reads glow intensity from red and the cyan map needs red set, with the colour coming from elsewhere. Run `python3 scripts/validate_extension.py --game "/path/to/X4 Foundations"` to also verify these external texture paths in the base-game catalogs. Run `blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/check_blender.py -- "/path/p1[assets]/andromeda/ship_and_xl_cruiser_01.blend"` to check the saved scene, longitudinal shields and emitted DAE.


The great arcs and the lower arms are rebuilt as parametric sweeps by `andromeda_arms.py`, after `smooth_crests` and before anything is placed; the right side is built and `symmetrize` mirrors it. Each arm is a Catmull-Rom path through nine control points (taken from the source arm's traced centreline) and a profile interpolated between five stations: a flattened oval, that is a rectangle whose short sides are semicircles, whose inner side narrows towards a point with the taper value. On the great arc the profile is oval up to S2, half way at S3 and a sharp inner triangle from S4 to the tip; it is 171 m wide at the root, about 88 m forward of S3, 45 m thick, narrowing to 60 × 40 m near the tip, and carries a rib 8 m wide and 5 m high along the middle of its top. The lower arm is an oval lying flat that thins towards the tip, from 140 × 60 m at its root in the flank through 90 × 55, 90 × 48 and 85 × 42 to 55 × 32 m. Both end in a 70 m pointed cone at the shared tip, taken off the end of the path so the ship keeps its length, and closed with a fan so no triangle is degenerate. To join an arm to the hull, the source arm is traced by marching from a seed inside it, recentring on 72 rays per section; it is cut along the station plane at 7% (arc) or 14% (lower arm) of its length with `bisect_plane`, faces of the old arm beyond the cut are removed when they lie inside its traced section plus 10 m, small islands left floating near it (fins) go too, and the old shared tip is cleared within 170 m. A loft of ten rings runs from the hull's own section at the cut, resampled at 72 equal angles, to the new profile 70–80 m further on, and the sweep continues from there. Every old arm is removed before any new one is built; otherwise the lower arm's removal cut into the new arc where the two meet. Every ring is resampled at the same 72 angles (enough to resolve the rib), so the loft and sweep stay one watertight tube. Toward the shared tip the two arms fuse into one pod instead of meeting as two cones. `build_pod` pairs each arc ring with the nearest lower-arm ring and takes, in a plane across both, the smooth union (polynomial smooth minimum of the two signed distances) of their sections, with a blend radius twice the thinner arm there, so a fillet grows where they come close and the tip stays sharp. The pod starts where that union first joins between the two centres, 3 m inside the arms, rises over the first fifth of its length to 1 m outside them, so no lip shows where it emerges, swells by up to 5 m along the middle, and ends on the farther of the two tips. Each ring is found by marching out from the middle at 72 angles to the union's edge.

Arm turrets follow the layout chosen with the user: on each side eight medium turrets on top of the arc, alternating 10 m either side of the crest, eight under the arc, and four under the lower arm, each with a medium shield 16 m towards the root; the top ones sit either side of the rib. Connection groups protect existing saves (`package_assets.py` refuses group changes), so the arm-up group keeps its ten turrets: the two beyond the eight on the arc sit on top of the lower arm. The arm-down group keeps its ten and gains `con_turret_155` to `_158` with shields `con_shieldgen_m_160` to `_163` for the last two places under each lower arm. The main weapons stay at the arm tips through `arm_tips`.

### Original integrated twin turrets — experimental, failed in-game

#### Differences from vanilla L turrets (2026-10-22, unverified in-game)

The component XML was compared with the base-game `turret_arg_l_beam_01_mk1`, `turret_arg_l_plasma_01_mk1`, `turret_tel_l_beam_01_mk1` and `turret_par_l_laser_01_mk1`, extracted read-only from `01.cat`. All four agree with each other and differ from the custom turret in the following ways. These are candidates to test one at a time, not established causes:

- The first connection is `container` with tags `contents` and value 0; the custom component has `space` with tags `turret`.
- The aim target is `con_aimtarget_01`, offset up to the yaw pivot height (13.3 m on the Argon beam); the custom `con_aimtarget` sits at the socket origin.
- The yaw and pitch parts are placed by connection offsets at their pivots; the custom parts have no offsets and carry their geometry from the origin.
- The socket connection is tagged `part animation` with activation animations; the custom one has only `part`. Argon, Teladi and Paranid turrets ship no `.ani` file, so the near-empty custom `.ani` alone is unlikely to be the cause.
- The custom macro adds `<weapon angle="1.4"/>`, which the Argon beam macro does not have.
- The custom turret is about 64 m wide with 75 m barrels, several times a vanilla L turret.
